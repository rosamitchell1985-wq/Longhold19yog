About Longhold Yoga

Who writes this, how the guides are made, what we will and will not claim, and exactly how the site pays for itself.

Why the site exists

Most yoga instruction online is either a video you cannot pause to read, or a page that tells you to "hold for a few breaths" and moves on. Neither is much use if you are practicing alone in a living room and want to know what your back foot should be doing.

Longhold Yoga is written instruction. Every pose has entry steps, alignment cues, a breath pattern with counts, a hold time in seconds, the mistakes people commonly make, at least two prop-supported options treated as equal alternatives, a progression, and a contraindication note. Every sequence has a stated duration, a difficulty, a prop list, a pose-by-pose order with hold times, and a written beginner path.

The name comes from hold time. The number attached to a pose is the difference between instruction and a suggestion.

How the guides are made

Every pose guide follows the same process, in the same order.

  1. Practice it, then teach it, then write it

    Nothing gets written from a book alone. Each pose is practiced across a range of setups, taught to people at different levels of experience, and only then written up, so the common mistakes section comes from watching people make them rather than from a list.

  2. Write the modifications first

    The chair, wall and prop options are drafted before the full version, not after it. This is deliberate. Writing modifications last produces modifications that read like afterthoughts, and that framing is exactly what we are trying to avoid.

  3. Strip every claim

    A separate editing pass removes anything that says or implies a pose treats, relieves, cures, prevents or improves a condition. What survives describes what physically happens: which joints move, which tissues lengthen, what has to hold you up.

  4. Check the Sanskrit

    Names are written with diacritics and checked against standard transliteration. The pronunciation guides are approximations in ordinary American English, and they are marked as approximations.

  5. Correct it when it is wrong

    If something here is inaccurate, tell us and it gets fixed, with a dated note on the page recording what changed. Corrections are not quietly edited in.

What this site will not publish

Written down so you can hold us to it.

  • No health, medical or therapeutic claims. No pose, sequence or breathing pattern here is described as treating, curing, healing, relieving or preventing any condition. Not back pain, not anxiety, not sleep, not digestion, not blood pressure, not immunity, not fertility. We describe what a pose does physically and stop there.
  • No weight-loss framing. No calorie figures, no fat-burning language, no body transformation, no before-and-after content, no toning claims, no commentary on body shape. Yoga is not presented here as a means of changing how a body looks.
  • No diet content. No cleanses, no fasting, no detox, no supplements, no meal plans. This is a movement education site and that is its entire scope.
  • No invented credentials, reviews or testimonials. Any teacher name, qualification, review or star rating that has not been verified is marked as a placeholder in the page source and does not appear as though it were real.
  • No fake urgency. No countdown timers, no invented scarcity, no expiring discounts, no "only three places left".
  • No unlabeled sponsorship. Any article paid for by a brand says so at the top of the page, above the first paragraph.

How this site is funded

All of it is free to read. That is paid for in six ways, and there is no seventh.

  • Display advertising. One labeled ad slot per page, placed below the main content. Not on legal pages and not on the 404 page. Advertising cookies are not set until you consent to them.
  • Membership, $6 per month or $54 per year in USD, which removes the advertising and adds downloadable sequence PDFs, saved practice collections, printable pose cards and early access to new guides. It never puts instruction behind a wall.
  • Affiliate commissions on mats, blocks, straps, blankets and bolsters, disclosed next to each link rather than only in the footer, with a free household substitute listed for every prop we recommend.
  • Paid digital products, such as the downloadable beginner program and the sequence bundles.
  • Sponsored content and brand partnerships, labeled as sponsored at the top of the page. We do not accept sponsorship from supplement, diet, cleanse or weight-loss businesses, and we do not publish sponsored content containing health claims.
  • The teacher directory, where a basic listing is free and a featured placement is paid and marked as featured.

Full detail, including the FTC-required affiliate language, is on the Advertising and Affiliate Disclosure page. The membership terms are on the Pricing page.
