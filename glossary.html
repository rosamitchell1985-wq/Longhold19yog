Glossary

Sixty Sanskrit and English terms you will meet in a class or on this site, with a plain pronunciation guide and a short definition. Diacritics are written throughout, because leaving them off changes the word.

How to read the pronunciation

The guides in brackets are approximations using ordinary American English sounds. They are close enough to be understood, not linguistically exact.

  • A line over a vowel (ā, ī, ū) means it is held about twice as long. Āsana starts with a long "ah", not a short "uh".
  • A dot under a letter (ṭ, ḍ, ṇ, ṣ) marks a retroflex sound, made with the tongue curled slightly further back. Saying the ordinary English version is fine.
  • ś and ṣ are both roughly "sh".
  • c is always "ch" as in chair, never "k" or "s".
  • Stress usually falls on a long vowel when there is one.

Nobody in a studio will mind if you get these wrong, and most teachers pronounce them somewhat differently from each other anyway.

Core vocabulary

Āsana (AH-suh-nuh)
Seat, or pose. The suffix on nearly every pose name. Originally referred specifically to a seat for sitting practice.
Vinyāsa (vin-YAH-suh)
To place in a special way. In practice, the linking of movement to breath, and the name of a whole class format.
Prāṇāyāma (prah-nah-YAH-muh)
Breathing practice. Usually translated as extension or regulation of the breath.
Prāṇa (PRAH-nuh)
Breath, or vital energy, depending on the text and the teacher.
Dṛṣṭi (DRISH-tee)
Gaze point. Where the eyes rest during a pose. Steadying the gaze steadies balance.
Bandha (BAHN-dah)
Lock or bind. A deliberate engagement in a specific area of the body, most often the pelvic floor or lower abdomen.
Mudrā (MOO-drah)
Seal or gesture, usually made with the hands.
Namaste (NUH-muh-stay)
A respectful greeting. Common at the close of a class in Western studios, less universal in India than many assume.
Om (ohm)
A syllable chanted at the start or end of some classes. Optional in every studio; sitting quietly through it is normal.
Śavāsana (shuh-VAH-suh-nuh)
Corpse Pose. The final rest, lying flat on the back doing nothing. Ends nearly every practice.
Yoga (YOH-guh)
From a root meaning to yoke or join. In common use, the whole practice, including but not limited to the physical poses.
Haṭha (HUT-huh)
Force or effort. Broadly covers physical yoga practice. Also a common class label.
Mysore (MY-sore)
A city in India, and by extension a class format where each student practices their own portion of a set sequence with individual attention.
Sūrya Namaskāra (SOOR-yuh nuh-muh-SKAH-ruh)
Sun Salutation. A short linked sequence of poses, repeated, that opens many classes.
Nāḍī (NAH-dee)
Channel. Used in the name of the breathing practice nāḍī śodhana, alternate nostril breathing.

Words that appear inside pose names

Learning these fifteen lets you decode most of the pose names you will meet, because Sanskrit pose names are descriptive compounds rather than arbitrary labels.

Adho (AH-doh)
Downward. As in Adho Mukha Śvānāsana, downward-facing dog.
Ūrdhva (OORD-vuh)
Upward. As in Ūrdhva Mukha Śvānāsana, upward-facing dog.
Mukha (MOO-kuh)
Face or facing.
Eka (AY-kuh)
One. As in Eka Pāda, one-legged.
Pāda (PAH-duh)
Foot or leg.
Hasta (HUS-tuh)
Hand.
Jānu (JAH-noo)
Knee.
Śīrṣa (SHEER-shuh)
Head. As in Jānu Śīrṣāsana, head-to-knee pose.
Pārśva (PAHRSH-vuh)
Side, or sideways.
Utthita (oot-TEE-tuh)
Extended.
Supta (SOOP-tuh)
Reclined, or sleeping. Marks the lying-down version of a pose.
Ardha (ARD-huh)
Half. Marks a shorter or partial version.
Baddha (BAHD-huh)
Bound.
Sālamba (SAH-lum-buh)
Supported, with a prop or with the arms.
Parivṛtta (puh-ree-VRIT-tuh)
Revolved, or twisted. Marks the twisting version of a standing pose.

Pose names you will hear

The twenty pose names most likely to be called out in a general-level class, in the order they usually appear.

Tāḍāsana (tah-DAH-suh-nuh)
Mountain Pose. Standing still with the weight even across both feet.
Bālāsana (bah-LAH-suh-nuh)
Child's Pose. Kneeling and folded forward. Full guide.
Mārjaryāsana (mar-jar-YAH-suh-nuh)
Cat Pose. Rounding the spine on hands and knees.
Bitilāsana (bee-tee-LAH-suh-nuh)
Cow Pose. The counterpart to Cat, letting the belly descend.
Adho Mukha Śvānāsana (AH-doh MOO-kuh shvah-NAH-suh-nuh)
Downward-Facing Dog. Full guide.
Uttānāsana (oo-tah-NAH-suh-nuh)
Standing Forward Fold.
Añjaneyāsana (ahn-jah-nay-AH-suh-nuh)
Low Lunge, with the back knee on the floor.
Vīrabhadrāsana II (veer-ah-bah-DRAH-suh-nuh)
Warrior II. Full guide.
Utthita Trikoṇāsana (oot-TEE-tuh trik-oh-NAH-suh-nuh)
Extended Triangle Pose.
Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana (oot-TEE-tuh parsh-vuh-koh-NAH-suh-nuh)
Extended Side Angle Pose.
Vṛkṣāsana (vrik-SHAH-suh-nuh)
Tree Pose. Full guide.
Utkaṭāsana (oot-kuh-TAH-suh-nuh)
Chair Pose. Sitting down onto a seat that is not there.
Phalakāsana (fuh-luh-KAH-suh-nuh)
Plank Pose.
Bhujaṅgāsana (boo-jung-GAH-suh-nuh)
Cobra Pose. Lifting the chest while lying face down.
Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana (SAY-too BAHN-dah sar-vahn-GAH-suh-nuh)
Bridge Pose. Full guide.
Paścimottānāsana (POSH-chee-moh-tah-NAH-suh-nuh)
Seated Forward Fold. Full guide.
Jānu Śīrṣāsana (JAH-noo sheer-SHAH-suh-nuh)
Head-to-Knee Forward Bend.
Baddha Koṇāsana (BAHD-huh koh-NAH-suh-nuh)
Bound Angle Pose. Soles of the feet together, knees wide.
Ardha Matsyendrāsana (ARD-huh mots-yen-DRAH-suh-nuh)
Half Lord of the Fishes. The standard seated twist.
Viparīta Karaṇī (vip-uh-REE-tuh kuh-ruh-NEE)
Legs Up the Wall. Often held for five to fifteen minutes.

English terms used on this site

Not Sanskrit, but worth defining because different sources use them differently.

Alignment cue
A specific instruction about where a body part goes or what it does in a pose. Distinct from an entry step, which is about getting into the shape.
Hold time
How long you stay in a pose. Given here in seconds or breaths, never as "a few breaths".
Contraindication
A reason to approach a pose with care or avoid it. Every pose guide on this site carries one.
Modification
A version of a pose set up differently, usually with a prop. Treated here as an equal alternative, not a lesser version.
Progression
A next step from a pose, once the current version is steady. Often more duration rather than more depth.
Counter-pose
A shape practiced after a strong one to move the spine or joints in a different direction. Child's Pose after a backbend, for example.
Prop
Anything that brings the floor closer, makes your arms longer, or holds your weight. Blocks, straps, blankets, bolsters, chairs and walls.
Restorative
A practice format using heavy prop support and holds of five to twenty minutes, with no muscular effort. More on styles.
Flow
Informal name for a Vinyasa class, or for a linked sequence within any class.
Level one
A studio label for a class suitable for beginners. Also written as fundamentals, foundations, gentle or all levels.

A note on the language

Sanskrit pose names are not decoration and they are not gatekeeping. They are descriptive: utthita means extended, pārśva means side, koṇa means angle, so Utthita Pārśvakoṇāsana is extended side angle pose. Once fifteen or so of these components are familiar, most pose names decode themselves.

Writing the diacritics is a matter of accuracy rather than formality. The line over the ā and the dot under the ṣ are part of the spelling, in the same way an accent is part of the spelling of a French word. Plenty of good sources leave them out, and this site does not, which is why some spellings here look different from spellings elsewhere.
