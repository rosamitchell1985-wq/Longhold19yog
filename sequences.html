Sequences

Four complete practices, from eight minutes in a desk chair to a forty-minute gentle session. Each one lists its total duration, its difficulty, the props it needs, and a pose-by-pose order with hold times. Each has a timer on the page and prints as a clean practice handout.

10-Minute Morning

  • 10 minutes
  • Beginner
  • Mat, wall

Ten poses in ten minutes. Moves the spine in every direction it has, opens the front of the hips after a night lying down, and puts weight through the hands and feet before the day starts. A seated path runs alongside it, pose for pose.

Desk Reset

  • 8 minutes
  • Beginner
  • Chair only

Twelve short movements done from a chair and beside a desk, in ordinary clothes, without getting on the floor. Built for the middle of a working day when there is no mat, no changing room and no eight-minute window that looks like a workout.

Evening Wind-Down

  • 20 minutes
  • Beginner
  • Bolster, block, wall

Ten supported holds of one to three minutes, entirely on the floor. Nothing here asks for strength or balance. The breath runs long on the exhale throughout, and it closes with legs up a wall.

Gentle Full Practice

  • 40 minutes
  • Beginner to intermediate
  • Mat, blocks, strap, blanket

Twenty-two entries across warm-up, standing work, floor work and a five-minute final rest. The full shape of a studio class without the drive, at a pace that leaves time to set each pose up properly rather than arriving in it.

How these sequences are built

Every sequence on this site follows the same six rules, which are worth knowing whether you use ours or write your own.

  • Warm before you load. Nothing that requires range of motion appears in the first two minutes. The spine moves before it is asked to hold a shape.
  • Both sides, always. Any asymmetrical pose is done on both sides for the same length of time, in the same order, every time.
  • Hold times are stated, not implied. "A few breaths" means different things to different people. Every pose here has a number attached.
  • A counter-shape follows a strong shape. A backbend is followed by something neutral or gently rounded, not by another backbend.
  • The last thing is rest. Every sequence ends lying down or sitting still, for at least thirty seconds and usually much longer.
  • There is a stated beginner path. Not a vague suggestion to modify, but a specific list of what to change and what to leave out.

Using the timer

Each sequence page has a timer that counts each pose's hold and moves to the next one automatically. It can be paused at any point, stepped forward or back, and reset. Tapping any row in the printed order jumps the timer directly to that pose, which is useful when you want to repeat something or skip past a shape that is not for you today.

The timer is a convenience, not a rule. Coming out of a pose early is always the correct decision if something hurts, and staying longer than the number says is fine when the pose is comfortable and your breath is even.

Printing a sequence

Use your browser's print command on any sequence page. The print stylesheet strips the header, the navigation, the timer controls, the advertising slot, the trailing commentary and the footer, leaving the pose order, the hold times, the prop list, the beginner path and the safety note. Depending on the sequence that is two or three sheets of letter paper, set at a size that stays readable from standing.

Health and safety

These sequences are general movement education, not medical advice, and no sequence here treats, prevents or manages any health condition. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. Stop if you feel pain, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. Read the full disclaimer.
