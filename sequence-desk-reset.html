Desk Reset

Twelve short movements done from a chair and beside a desk, in ordinary clothes, without getting on the floor. Built for the middle of a working day when there is no mat and no changing room.

Total duration
8 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Props required
A sturdy chair without wheels, a desk or wall
Props optional
A strap or belt, a cushion for the seat
Breath pattern
Inhale 4, exhale 6 throughout
Clothing
Whatever you are already wearing
Before you start

Use a chair that does not roll or swivel. If your office chair has castors, wheel it against a wall, sit on a different chair, or lock the castors. Two of the movements below shift your weight forward, and a chair that moves underneath you is the one avoidable hazard in this sequence.

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Seated tall breathing Sit forward on the seat so your back is off the chair back. Both feet flat, knees over ankles. Inhale four counts, exhale six.
    45 seconds
  2. Neck release, right side Drop your right ear toward your right shoulder and let your left arm hang heavy. Do not pull with the hand. Breathe.
    35 seconds
  3. Neck release, left side Second side. Keep both sitting bones evenly on the seat so the movement stays in the neck rather than the whole torso.
    35 seconds
  4. Shoulder rolls Slow full circles, as large as you can make them. Roughly five backward, then five forward. Let the breath stay even.
    30 seconds
  5. Seated Cat and Cow Mārjaryāsana and Bitilāsana Hands on your knees. Inhale to open the chest and let the belly descend, exhale to round the spine and drop the head.
    45 seconds
  6. Seated twist, right Ardha Matsyendrāsana Both feet stay flat and both sitting bones stay down. Turn from the base of the spine upward. Hands rest lightly, they do not lever you around.
    40 seconds
  7. Seated twist, left Ardha Matsyendrāsana Second side. Same restraint with the hands. If the knees have drifted apart, square them first.
    40 seconds
  8. Seated figure four, right Supta Kapotāsana, seated Cross the right ankle over the left thigh and let the right knee fall out. Sit tall first, then hinge forward from the hips only as far as is comfortable.
    45 seconds
  9. Seated figure four, left Supta Kapotāsana, seated Second side. Keep the spine long as you hinge forward. If the ankle will not cross the thigh, rest it on the shin instead.
    45 seconds
  10. Chair-supported forward fold Uttānāsana, seated Feet wide, fold forward between the knees, arms and head loose. Come up slowly, stacking one vertebra at a time.
    45 seconds
  11. Standing chest opener at the desk Ardha Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Stand, put both hands flat on the desk edge and walk your feet back until your torso is horizontal and your arms are straight. Let the chest descend between the arms.
    45 seconds
  12. Seated closing breath Sit back down, feet flat, hands anywhere. Inhale four counts, exhale six, about four times. Then go back to what you were doing.
    30 seconds

Beginner modification path

The whole sequence is already chair-based, so the modifications here are about reducing range rather than changing position.

  • Neck releases: reduce the tilt by half. Neck range is highly individual and there is no target angle.
  • Seated Cat and Cow: keep the movement small and let it happen mostly in the upper back if the lower back is sensitive.
  • Seated twist: keep both hands on your own thighs rather than using the chair. The twist should come from turning, not from pulling.
  • Seated figure four: if the ankle will not cross the opposite thigh, rest it on the shin or simply let one knee fall open with the foot still on the floor. Do not hinge forward at all if the hip is sensitive.
  • Chair forward fold: fold only to horizontal, with your forearms on your thighs. Come up on an inhale.
  • Desk chest opener: stand closer to the desk so the torso is at forty-five degrees rather than horizontal. Skip it entirely if you have shoulder pain.

Notes on practicing this one

It is designed to be unremarkable to look at. Nothing here requires you to get on the floor, change clothes, or explain to a colleague what you are doing. The two standing movements look like stretching at a desk, because that is what they are. If you work in an open office, this matters more than any other design consideration.

Twice a day beats once a week. Eight minutes at eleven and again at three is a completely different proposition from a forty-minute session on Saturday, and it is much easier to protect in a calendar. Two shorter blocks also break up sitting time twice rather than once.

Set the chair up first. Feet flat on the floor, knees roughly level with the hips, back away from the chair back. If your feet do not reach the floor, put a stack of books or a box under them. If the seat is too low, sit on a folded jacket. These take four seconds and change every movement that follows.

Ordinary clothes are fine, shoes are optional. Everything here works in shoes. If you can take them off for the standing desk opener, the feet grip the floor better, but nothing in the sequence depends on it.

If the middle of your day is the only reliable window you have, this sequence and the evening wind-down together make a workable weekly practice with no mornings involved at all.
