10-Minute Morning

Ten poses in ten minutes, done before coffee. It moves the spine in every direction it has, opens the front of the hips after a night of lying down, and puts weight through the hands and feet.

Total duration
10 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Props required
A mat or firm floor, a wall, one folded blanket
Props optional
Two blocks, a chair for the seated path
Breath pattern
Even breath throughout. Inhale 4, exhale 4.
Best time
Within an hour of waking, before eating

Sequence timer

Seated centering

Pose 1 of 10

1:00

The timer moves on automatically when a hold ends. Tap any row below to jump straight to that pose. Coming out early is always the right call if something hurts.

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Seated centering Sukhāsana Sit on the folded blanket so your hips are higher than your knees. Even breath, hands anywhere comfortable.
    60 seconds
  2. Cat and Cow Mārjaryāsana and Bitilāsana On hands and knees. Inhale to let the belly descend and the chest open, exhale to round the spine. Move at breath speed.
    90 seconds
  3. Child's Pose Bālāsana Knees as wide as the mat, hips back, forehead on the floor or a block. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  4. Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Keep the knees generously bent. Length in the spine comes before straight legs. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  5. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana Back knee down on the folded blanket, front foot flat, hips settling forward. Hands on blocks or on the front thigh.
    60 seconds
  6. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana Same setup on the second side. The two sides will feel different. That is information, not a problem to fix this morning.
    60 seconds
  7. Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana Feet hip-width, knees soft. Let the head hang heavy. Hands on blocks, on the shins, or holding opposite elbows.
    60 seconds
  8. Mountain Pose, arms overhead Tāḍāsana Feet hip-width apart, weight even across both feet. Reach the arms up without lifting the shoulders or flaring the lower ribs.
    45 seconds
  9. Tree Pose, right foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Stand beside the wall with two fingertips touching it. Foot to the ankle, calf or inner thigh. Never on the knee. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  10. Tree Pose, left foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Second side, same wall contact. When the timer ends, stand still with both feet down for a few breaths before you move into the day.
    45 seconds

Beginner modification path

If any of the poses above are not available today, this is the version to run instead. It is the same sequence with the same order and the same timing, and it is not a lesser practice.

  • Seated centering: sit on a chair with both feet flat instead of on the floor.
  • Cat and Cow: do it seated on the chair, rounding and opening the spine with the hands on the thighs.
  • Child's Pose: stay seated and fold forward over a cushion on your lap, forehead resting on it.
  • Downward-Facing Dog: use the wall version. Hands flat on the wall at hip height, feet walked back until the torso is horizontal.
  • Low Lunge: stand facing a chair, put one foot up on the seat and step the other foot back, keeping both hands on the chair back.
  • Standing Forward Fold: sit on the chair and fold forward over the thighs with the hands on the shins.
  • Mountain Pose: do it seated, reaching the arms up from the chair.
  • Tree Pose: use the seated version, crossing one ankle over the opposite thigh and letting the knee fall out to the side.

Notes on practicing this one

Ten minutes is not a compromise length. It is long enough to move every joint the sequence intends to move and short enough that you will actually do it four times a week. Four ten-minute practices teach a body more than one forty-minute practice on a Sunday.

Do it before you eat if you can. Forward folds and lunges are more comfortable on an empty stomach. If that does not fit your morning, leave thirty to sixty minutes after a light breakfast.

Mornings are stiffer, and that is normal. Spinal discs take on fluid overnight, so most people have less range first thing. Keep the knees bent in the folds, do not measure your morning range against your evening range, and let the first two poses be genuinely gentle.

Print it and leave it out. The single most effective thing for keeping a morning practice going is not motivation. It is the mat already unrolled and the sheet of paper already on the floor beside it. Use your browser's print command on this page.

When this sequence stops feeling like an event, the forty-minute gentle full practice is the natural next step, and the desk reset slots into the middle of the day without needing a mat at all.
