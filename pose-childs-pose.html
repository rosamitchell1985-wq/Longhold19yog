Child's Pose

Bālāsana  (BAH-lah-suh-nuh)

Kneeling and folded forward over the thighs, forehead supported. Bāla means child. It is the shape a teacher will point to when they say "rest whenever you need to," and it is worth knowing well enough that resting is genuinely restful.

Pose breakdown

Bālāsana

  • Beginner
  • Hips
  • Spine
  • No prop required
Difficulty
Beginner. Knee and ankle comfort decides the setup, not strength.
Breath pattern
Extended exhale. Inhale 4 counts, exhale 8 counts.
Suggested hold
1 to 3 minutes, longer when fully propped
What moves
Hip flexion, knee flexion, ankle length, lengthening along the back of the spine
Props that help
A bolster, one or two blankets, a block, a chair
Where it appears
10-Minute Morning, Evening Wind-Down, Gentle Full Practice
Entry steps
  • Kneel on the mat with your shins on the floor and the tops of your feet down.
  • Bring your big toes to touch behind you.
  • Choose your knee width. Knees together makes a narrower shape with more pressure on the belly. Knees as wide as the mat leaves room at the front of the body and is usually the more comfortable choice.
  • Send your hips back toward your heels as far as they comfortably travel. They do not need to arrive.
  • Walk your hands forward and lower your forehead toward the mat, a stacked fist, a block or a bolster.
  • Choose an arm position: reaching forward for more length through the sides, or alongside the body with the palms up for more release across the shoulders.
  • Let the shoulders soften, unclench the jaw and let the breath go long.
Alignment cues
  • The forehead is supported, not hanging. If it does not reach the floor, raise the floor with a block or a folded blanket. A hanging head keeps the neck working for the whole hold.
  • Sit bones travel back, rather than the chest pressing down. The direction is backward, not downward.
  • Tops of the feet on the mat, with the ankles long. If the ankles complain, roll a blanket and place it underneath them.
  • Shoulders away from the ears, whichever arm position you choose.
  • Breath into the back. With the front of the body folded, the breath naturally travels into the back of the ribs. Let it.
  • Nothing gripping. If any muscle is holding you in position, add a prop under it.
Common mistakes
  • Forcing the hips to the heels. They arrive when hip and knee proportions allow. Forcing it lifts the knees off the mat and puts strain across the knee joint.
  • Unsupported forehead. The most common one, and the reason people say Child's Pose is not restful. Bring the floor up.
  • Knees too close for the body. If there is compression at the front of the hips or the belly, take the knees wider.
  • Ignoring the ankles. Ankle discomfort here is common and completely fixable with a rolled blanket underneath them.
  • Treating it as a break rather than a pose. One to three minutes of long exhales is doing something. Two seconds on the way to the next thing is not.
  • Holding the shoulders up. If your arms are reaching forward and your shoulders are near your ears, walk the hands wider or bring the arms back alongside the body.
Accessible modifications

All of these are complete versions. The bolster version in particular is the one most experienced practitioners choose when they want to stay for five minutes.

Over a bolster

Place a bolster lengthwise between your knees with one short end near your hips, then lay your torso along it and turn your head to one side. Change the head direction halfway through. This holds the entire weight of the torso, which is what makes a five to ten minute hold possible. Two firm pillows in a pillowcase work.

Blanket behind the knees

Roll a blanket and place it in the crease behind your knees before you sit back. This reduces how far the knees have to bend and makes the pose available to a great many people who otherwise find it uncomfortable. Add a second roll under the ankles if they need it.

Block under the forehead

Set a block at any height directly under your forehead. The head is heavy, and giving it somewhere to land is the difference between resting and holding a position. A stacked fist or a folded towel does the same job.

Seated at a chair or table

If kneeling is not available at all, sit on a chair, place a cushion on your lap or a folded blanket on a table in front of you, and fold forward to rest your forehead on it with the arms loose. You get the same forward fold, the same long exhale and the same rest, with no load on the knees or ankles.

Progression

Progression here is duration and stillness rather than depth. Work from one minute to three, then from three to five with a bolster. When five minutes is genuinely comfortable, the next step is Sphinx Pose or a supported forward fold held for a similar length, or moving into the same shape with the arms walked over to one side to lengthen through the ribs on the opposite side, changing after ninety seconds. Depth is not the axis this pose improves along.

Contraindications and cautions

Approach with care or use the chair version if you have a knee injury or a recent knee replacement, an ankle injury, a hip injury or replacement where deep flexion is restricted, or if you are pregnant, in which case the wide-knee version with a bolster gives room at the front of the body. Come out of the pose if you feel sharp sensation in a knee, pins and needles in the feet, or pressure in the head. If you have recently had abdominal surgery, choose the seated version instead.

The pose people skip

In a group class, Child's Pose is offered roughly every ten minutes and taken by almost nobody. There is a social element to that which is worth naming: sitting out feels like being seen sitting out.

It is also the pose that most rewards being set up carefully. Almost everyone who says they find Child's Pose uncomfortable is doing one of three things: kneeling with an unsupported forehead, keeping the knees too close together for their hip proportions, or asking bent knees to fold further than they currently fold. Every one of those has a prop that fixes it in about four seconds.

Set up properly, it is the shape you can stay in for five minutes with a long exhale, and that is a genuinely different experience from the fifteen-second version taken between rounds of something harder. If you build a home practice, this is the pose to give real time to.
