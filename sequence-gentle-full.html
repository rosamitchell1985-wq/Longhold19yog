Gentle Full Practice

Twenty-two entries across warm-up, standing work, floor work and a five-minute final rest. The full shape of a studio class, at a pace that leaves time to set each pose up rather than arrive in it.

Total duration
40 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner to intermediate
Props required
A mat, two blocks, a strap, one folded blanket, a clear stretch of wall
Props optional
A bolster, a second blanket, a sturdy chair
Breath pattern
Even breath during standing work. Inhale 4, exhale 6 on the floor.
Structure
8 minutes warm-up, 15 minutes standing, 12 minutes floor, 5 minutes rest
Before you start

This is the longest sequence on the site and the only one that covers a full arc. It assumes you have practiced the ten-minute morning sequence a few times and know what Downward-Facing Dog and Warrior II ask of you. If you have not, start there instead. Nothing is lost by arriving here in a month.

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Seated centering Sukhāsana Sit on the folded blanket, hips higher than the knees. Inhale four, exhale four. Two minutes is longer than it sounds, which is the point.
    2 minutes
  2. Reclined knee hugs Apānāsana Lie on your back and draw both knees toward your chest. Make small slow circles with the knees, both directions.
    90 seconds
  3. Cat and Cow Mārjaryāsana and Bitilāsana On hands and knees with the blanket under the knees. Move at breath speed for the full two minutes, not faster.
    2 minutes
  4. Child's Pose Bālāsana Knees as wide as the mat, forehead on the floor or a block. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  5. Downward-Facing Dog Adho Mukha Śvānāsana Knees generously bent, spine long. Spend the first half of it walking the heels alternately if that suits you. Full guide.
    2 minutes
  6. Standing Forward Fold Uttānāsana Walk your feet toward your hands and hang. Knees soft, head heavy, hands on blocks or on the shins.
    90 seconds
  7. Mountain Pose Tāḍāsana Roll up one vertebra at a time. Stand still, feet hip-width, and notice where your weight actually sits before you move on.
    90 seconds
  8. Warrior II, right foot forward Vīrabhadrāsana II Wide stance, front knee tracking over the second toe, back outer heel firmly down, torso upright between the legs. Full guide.
    90 seconds
  9. Warrior II, left foot forward Vīrabhadrāsana II Second side. Match the stance width and the depth of the knee bend to the first side rather than to how it feels.
    90 seconds
  10. Triangle, right Utthita Trikoṇāsana Straighten the front leg without locking it, then tip sideways over it. Lower hand on a block on the inside or outside of the shin, whichever keeps the chest open.
    60 seconds
  11. Triangle, left Utthita Trikoṇāsana Second side. The block can be at a different height here than it was on the first side. That is ordinary.
    60 seconds
  12. Tree Pose, right foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Stand beside the wall with two fingertips on it. Foot to the ankle, calf or inner thigh. Full guide.
    60 seconds
  13. Tree Pose, left foot lifted Vṛkṣāsana Second side. One side is almost always steadier than the other, and that gap narrows with weeks, not within a session.
    60 seconds
  14. Low Lunge, right foot forward Añjaneyāsana Back knee down on the folded blanket, hands on blocks either side of the front foot. Let the hips settle forward while the chest stays lifted.
    90 seconds
  15. Low Lunge, left foot forward Añjaneyāsana Second side. This is the last of the standing work, so let it be unhurried before you come to the floor.
    90 seconds
  16. Seated Forward Fold Paścimottānāsana Sit on the front edge of the folded blanket, strap around the balls of both feet, knees softly bent. Inhale to lengthen, exhale to travel. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  17. Seated twist, right Ardha Matsyendrāsana Still on the blanket. Both sitting bones stay down. Inhale to lengthen, exhale to turn, starting from the base of the spine.
    90 seconds
  18. Seated twist, left Ardha Matsyendrāsana Second side. The hands rest where they land. They are not there to crank you further around.
    90 seconds
  19. Bridge Pose Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana Feet hip-width and close enough that your fingertips graze your heels. Lift from the legs, not the lower back. Keep the head still. Full guide.
    90 seconds
  20. Reclined twist, right Supta Matsyendrāsana Knees drop to the right, supported by a block or bolster if needed so that both shoulders can stay on the floor.
    2 minutes
  21. Reclined twist, left Supta Matsyendrāsana Second side. When it ends, take your time arranging blankets and props for the final rest rather than dropping straight into it.
    2 minutes
  22. Final rest Śavāsana Lie flat with a rolled blanket under the knees, arms a little away from the sides, palms up. Cover yourself if you are cool. Five minutes, doing nothing.
    5 minutes

Beginner modification path

Run this version for the first several times through, or any time you want the shape of the practice without the standing demand. It keeps the same order and the same timings.

  • Reclined knee hugs: keep the feet on the floor and draw one knee at a time toward the chest.
  • Cat and Cow: do it seated on a chair with the hands on the thighs if hands and knees is not comfortable.
  • Child's Pose: use a bolster lengthwise between the knees, or fold forward over a cushion while seated on a chair.
  • Downward-Facing Dog: use the wall version. Hands flat on the wall at hip height, feet walked back until the torso is horizontal.
  • Standing Forward Fold: fold only halfway, hands on the thighs, back flat.
  • Warrior II: bend the front knee to forty-five degrees rather than ninety, or use the seated chair version.
  • Triangle: rest the lower hand on the seat of a chair placed beside the front foot rather than on a block.
  • Tree Pose: use the kickstand version with the toes of the lifted foot still on the floor, fingertips on the wall.
  • Low Lunge: stand facing a chair, one foot on the seat, both hands on the chair back.
  • Seated Forward Fold: sit on two folded blankets, use the strap, and keep the knees well bent.
  • Bridge Pose: lift the hips only an inch or two, or place a block under the pelvis and rest on it instead.
  • Final rest: put a bolster under the knees and a folded blanket under the head. If lying flat is not comfortable, rest on your left side with a cushion between the knees.

Notes on practicing this one

Forty minutes is a commitment, so protect it properly. Put the phone in another room or in do-not-disturb. Lay out the two blocks, the strap and the blanket before you start. The single most common way a long home practice ends early is a notification arriving during the seated forward fold.

The five-minute final rest is not optional padding. It is roughly one eighth of the practice and it is the part most likely to get cut. Set an alarm for it if you are worried about the time, so you can stop watching the clock.

Expect the sides to differ. In a sequence this long you will do eight poses on both sides, and you will notice differences in most of them. Some of that is habitual, some is structural, and none of it needs correcting during the session. Note it and carry on.

Once a week is a reasonable frequency. This sequence is not designed to be done daily. A workable weekly shape is three or four ten-minute mornings, one or two desk resets, one evening wind-down, and this practice once, on the day you are least rushed.

Print it. This one in particular benefits from a sheet of paper at the edge of the mat, because twenty-two entries are more than most people want to hold in their head while also breathing evenly.
