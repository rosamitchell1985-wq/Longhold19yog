Seated Forward Fold

Paścimottānāsana  (POSH-chee-moh-tah-NAH-suh-nuh)

Sitting with the legs long in front and folding forward from the hips. Paścima means west, which in the older texts refers to the back of the body, the side that faces the setting sun when you face east at dawn. It is the pose most often practiced wrong, and a strap fixes nearly all of it.

Pose breakdown

Paścimottānāsana

  • Intermediate
  • Hamstrings
  • Spine
  • Strap recommended
Difficulty
Intermediate. Sitting upright with straight legs is itself the first hurdle.
Breath pattern
Inhale 4 counts to lengthen, exhale 6 counts to fold. Never fold on an inhale.
Suggested hold
1 to 2 minutes, or 5 to 8 breaths in a moving practice
What moves
Hip flexion, hamstring and calf length, lengthening along the whole back line
Props that help
A strap, one or two folded blankets, a bolster on the thighs, a chair
Where it appears
Gentle Full Practice
Entry steps
  • Sit on the floor with your legs straight out in front of you, feet hip-width apart.
  • Check the pelvis. If your lower back rounds backward the moment you sit down, sit on the front edge of one or two folded blankets until you can sit upright without effort. This step is not optional for most people.
  • Flex your feet gently so the toes point up and the kneecaps face the ceiling.
  • Let the knees be softly bent. Straight legs are not a requirement and a soft knee is not a compromise.
  • Loop a strap around the balls of both feet and hold one end in each hand.
  • Inhale and lift through the crown of your head to make the spine as long as it will go.
  • Exhale and hinge forward from the hip joints, walking your hands down the strap only as far as the spine stays long.
  • Stop at the point where the lower back starts to round. That point is the pose.
Alignment cues
  • Hinge at the hip, not the waist. Picture the fold happening at the crease where the thigh meets the torso.
  • Chest toward the feet, not the nose toward the knees. Leading with the head is what produces the rounded version.
  • Sit bones back and down. The pelvis has to tip forward for the fold to come from the hips. If it cannot, sit higher.
  • Knees soft. Bending the knees lets the pelvis tip. Straightening them at the cost of the spine trades the useful part of the pose for the appearance of it.
  • Shoulders down. Pulling on the strap with the shoulders up near the ears adds nothing.
  • Fold on the exhale. Inhale to lengthen, exhale to travel. Never the other way around.
Common mistakes
  • Sitting flat on the floor with a tipped-back pelvis. The single most common error. Almost everyone needs to sit on something.
  • Hauling on the feet. Grabbing the toes and pulling rounds the spine and turns a hip hinge into a spinal flexion.
  • Locking the knees. Straight legs are a result, not an instruction. Locked knees block the pelvis from tipping.
  • Bouncing. Repeatedly pulsing deeper is not how tissue lengthens and it does raise the chance of overdoing it.
  • Measuring by how far the hands reach. Reach distance is determined mostly by limb proportions. It says almost nothing about the quality of the pose.
  • Holding the breath at the bottom. If the breath stops, come up an inch until it starts again.
Accessible modifications

All four are complete versions. The supported one is what a restorative teacher would give you and it is worth five full minutes.

Strap around the feet

Loop a 6-foot strap around the balls of both feet, hold an end in each hand and keep your elbows soft. The strap gives your arms a length they do not have, so your spine never has to round in order to reach. A bathrobe belt or a long towel works identically.

Sitting on blankets

Sit on the front edge of one or two firm folded blankets so your hips are higher than your knees. This lets the pelvis tip forward, which is the entire mechanism of the pose. Most people need between two and six inches of lift, and needing more is a proportion fact, not a limitation.

Bolster on the thighs

Place a bolster or a stack of folded blankets across your thighs and rest your torso and forehead on it. Nothing is held. Stay for three to five minutes with a long exhale. This is the version to choose in an evening practice.

Seated in a chair

Sit forward on a sturdy chair with both feet flat and hip-width apart. Inhale to lengthen, exhale and hinge forward over your thighs, letting your hands and forearms rest on your thighs, on a second chair or on a stack of books. Same hinge, same breath, no floor involved.

Progression

Progress by lowering the height you sit on rather than by reaching further. When you can sit upright on a single folded blanket with an even pelvis, try a thinner fold, then the bare floor. Only after that does it make sense to work on the depth of the fold. From here, Head-to-Knee Forward Bend introduces the same hinge with one leg folded in, which lets you work one side at a time and usually reveals a noticeable difference between the two.

Contraindications and cautions

Approach with care, keep the knees well bent, or choose the chair version if you have a disc injury or a history of lower back injury, sciatic symptoms, a recent hamstring strain, a hip injury or replacement, or if you are pregnant, in which case taking the legs wider gives room at the front of the body. Come out of the pose if you feel sharp sensation behind the knee, at the sitting bones, or anything traveling down the back of the leg.

Why your hands do not need to reach your feet

How far forward you fold in this pose is determined by several things you did not choose: the length of your femurs relative to your torso, the depth of your hip sockets, the shape of the top of your thigh bone, and how far your pelvis can tip before the spine has to take over. Two people with identical hamstring length can end up eight inches apart.

None of that changes what the pose is for. The useful part is the hinge at the hip with a long spine, held with an even breath. That happens at three inches of travel and at eighteen. It stops happening the moment the spine rounds to buy distance, which is precisely when the pose starts looking more impressive.

A strap is the simplest way to keep the honest version. It removes the reason to round: your arms are now long enough. What you lose is the visual of a folded-in-half body, which was never the thing doing the work.
