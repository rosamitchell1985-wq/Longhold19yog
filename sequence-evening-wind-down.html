Evening Wind-Down

Ten supported holds of one to three minutes, entirely on the floor. Nothing here asks for strength or balance, and it closes with three minutes of legs resting up a wall.

Total duration
20 minutes
Difficulty
Beginner
Props required
A mat or carpet, a bolster or two firm cushions, one block, one blanket, a clear stretch of wall
Props optional
A second blanket, an eye covering, a strap
Breath pattern
Extended exhale throughout. Inhale 4, exhale 8.
Best time
Any time in the last two hours of your day
Set up before you start

Put every prop within reach of the mat before the first pose. In a sequence with holds this long, getting up to fetch a block is the thing most likely to end the practice early. You need the bolster, the block, the blanket and a clear patch of wall about three feet wide.

Sequence timer

Supported Child's Pose

Pose 1 of 10

3:00

Holds here are long. If a shape stops being comfortable before the timer ends, come out. The number is a guide, not an instruction.

Pose-by-pose order

  1. Supported Child's Pose Bālāsana Bolster lengthwise between wide knees, torso resting along it, head turned to one side. Turn your head the other way at the halfway mark. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  2. Seated side bend, right Pārśva Sukhāsana Sit cross-legged on the folded blanket. Right forearm or hand down beside you, left arm reaching up and over. Keep both sitting bones down.
    60 seconds
  3. Seated side bend, left Pārśva Sukhāsana Change which shin sits in front, then repeat on the second side. Reach long rather than leaning far.
    60 seconds
  4. Reclined figure four, right Supta Kapotāsana On your back, right ankle crossed over the left thigh. Hold behind the left thigh with both hands or loop a strap around it. Keep your head and shoulders on the floor.
    2 minutes
  5. Reclined figure four, left Supta Kapotāsana Second side. If the hip has a lot to say, leave the bottom foot on the floor and simply let the top knee fall open.
    2 minutes
  6. Supported Bridge Setu Bandha Sarvāṅgāsana Knees bent, feet flat. Lift the hips, slide the block underneath the bony part of the pelvis at its lowest height, then let your whole weight down onto it. Full guide.
    3 minutes
  7. Reclined twist, right Supta Matsyendrāsana Draw both knees in, then let them drop to the right with the bolster underneath or between them. If a shoulder lifts off the floor, add height under the knees.
    90 seconds
  8. Reclined twist, left Supta Matsyendrāsana Second side with the same support. Turn your head away from the knees only if your neck is comfortable doing so. It is optional.
    90 seconds
  9. Legs up the wall Viparīta Karaṇī Sit sideways beside the wall, then swing your legs up as you lie back. Hips a few inches from the wall, folded blanket under your head, arms wide. Do nothing at all.
    3 minutes
  10. Final rest Śavāsana Slide away from the wall and lie flat. Legs long, feet falling open, arms a little away from the sides, palms up. Let the breath return to its own rhythm.
    2 minutes

Beginner modification path

Long holds are more demanding of comfort than of capability. Every change below is about adding support so that stillness is actually possible.

  • Supported Child's Pose: if kneeling is not available, lie on your back with your calves resting on a chair seat instead, knees bent at ninety degrees.
  • Seated side bend: sit on a chair rather than the floor, both feet flat, and do the same reach.
  • Reclined figure four: use a strap around the bottom thigh so you are not holding with the arms for two minutes. Or leave the bottom foot on the floor entirely.
  • Supported Bridge: use a bolster or a stack of two folded blankets instead of a block if the block feels hard under the pelvis. If lying on your back is not comfortable, replace this with two more minutes of the reclined twist.
  • Reclined twist: put the bolster fully under both knees so the legs are supported the whole way. Nothing should be held.
  • Legs up the wall: if getting the legs up the wall is awkward, rest the calves on a chair seat instead. It is a genuine substitute, not a lesser one.
  • Final rest: put a bolster or rolled blanket under the knees. This is what most people find comfortable and it is a standard setup rather than an accommodation.
Health and safety

This sequence is general movement education, not medical advice. It does not treat, prevent or manage any health condition, and it makes no claim about sleep. Speak with a qualified healthcare professional before beginning a new physical practice, particularly if you are pregnant, recently injured, post-surgical or managing a health condition. If you are pregnant, extended time lying flat on the back is generally not advised; lie propped on your left side instead for the resting poses. Stop if you feel pain, and work with a qualified teacher for hands-on guidance. Read the full health and safety disclaimer.

Notes on practicing this one

Long holds are a different skill from short ones. Three minutes in a shape is several times as long as anything in the morning sequence, and the experience is not simply more of the same thing. Around the ninety-second mark most people meet a strong urge to move. That is worth noticing rather than obeying immediately, and it is also worth obeying if the sensation is sharpening rather than settling.

Support everything. In an active practice a small amount of muscular holding is part of the pose. Here it is the thing to eliminate. If any muscle is working to keep you in position, put something underneath it. A shape you can hold for three minutes without effort is the shape this sequence wants.

Warmth matters more than you expect. Body temperature drops when you stop moving, and a person who is slightly cold cannot get comfortable in a three-minute hold. Wear a layer more than feels necessary, or keep a spare blanket to pull over you for the last two poses.

The extended exhale is the whole breath instruction. Inhale for four, exhale for eight, for the duration. If eight is a strain, use six. The breath-pacing bar under the header on this page is already set to this pattern; press Start pacing and follow it. More on breath counting.

It pairs well with the desk reset. If mornings are not available to you, this sequence plus the eight-minute desk reset makes a complete weekly practice with no early starts involved.
