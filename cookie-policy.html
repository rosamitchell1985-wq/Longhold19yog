Every category of cookie this site uses, what each one does, how long it lasts, who sets it, and how to change your mind at any time.

Effective date: 08/27/2026  ·  Last updated: 08/27/2026

What a cookie is

A cookie is a small text file a website asks your browser to store. On the next visit the browser sends it back, which is how a site can remember something between page loads. Related technologies such as local storage do the same job by different means, and everything on this page applies to those too.

This site uses local storage rather than a cookie to remember your consent choice, because it does not need to be sent to a server. It is treated as strictly necessary for the same reasons a consent cookie would be.

Nothing non-essential loads before you choose

On your first visit a banner appears at the bottom of the screen with three options: Accept all, Reject non-essential, and Manage preferences. The banner does not cover the content of the page and does not block you from reading or navigating.

Until you choose one, no analytics tag and no advertising tag is loaded and no cookie beyond the strictly necessary category is set. Choosing Reject non-essential is a real rejection: those tags never load at all for that visit, rather than loading in a limited mode.

If you take no action, nothing non-essential loads and the banner appears again on your next visit.

Opting out at the vendor

As well as the settings on this site, you can opt out directly with advertising vendors:

  • Google Ads Settings, to turn off personalized advertising from Google.
  • aboutads.info, the Digital Advertising Alliance opt-out for participating vendors.
  • Network Advertising Initiative opt-out, covering many vendors at once.

If you are a California resident, opting out of advertising cookies here is also how you exercise your right to opt out of the sharing of personal information for cross-context behavioral advertising. See the CCPA and CPRA section of the Privacy Policy. We honor the Global Privacy Control browser signal where your browser sends one.

Controlling cookies in your browser

Every major browser lets you view, block and delete cookies, usually under Settings, then Privacy. Blocking all cookies will stop the strictly necessary ones working, which means the consent banner will reappear on every page and any membership session will not persist. Blocking third-party cookies only is a reasonable middle setting and does not break this site.

Your browser's private or incognito mode discards all cookies when the window closes, which also means your consent choice is forgotten each time.

Fonts, images and other requests

This site requests its typefaces from Google Fonts and its photographs from an image content delivery network. Those requests do not set advertising cookies, but the services do receive your IP address as a technical necessity of delivering the file. There is no way to serve a remote file without the remote server seeing the request.

Changes to this policy

If we add a cookie or change what an existing one does, this page is updated and, where the change requires it, the consent banner is shown again so you can make a fresh choice. The "last updated" date at the top always reflects the current version.
